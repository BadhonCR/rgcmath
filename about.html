History of the College

Rangpur Government College was established on 25 July 1963 in response to the growing demand for higher education after the nationalization of Carmichael College on 01 January 1963, which significantly limited student admission opportunities in Rangpur. The college was founded through the dedicated efforts of Professor Emaz Uddin along with the generous support of Professor Fulae Hossain, DC O. M. Karni, ADC Abdus Sattar, District Education Officer Tahmedur Rahman, Advocate Abul Khan, Amin Hossain, Abu Salek, Abul Hossain, Moin Uddin Sarkar MP, and many distinguished citizens who donated land and funds.

Professor Emaz Uddin was appointed as the first Principal, while Professor Fulae Hossain served as the first Vice Principal. Initially, the college operated as a night college at Koilash Ranjan High School and as a day college at the Zamindarbari (Kalidham) of Ram Babu. It was affiliated with the University of Rajshahi from its inception until 1992. Beginning with only the Intermediate Arts (IA) programme, the college introduced BA courses in 1965 and B.Sc. programmes in 1970 due to its excellent academic reputation.

In 1967, the college shifted to its permanent campus, and a boys' hostel was established in the former Kalidham premises. During the Liberation War of 1971, the college actively participated in the national struggle. One of its students, Moslem Uddin, embraced martyrdom, and the boys' hostel was later named "Shaheed Moslem Uddin Hall" in his honour.

The college was nationalized on 01 November 1984. Since then, student enrolment has grown rapidly, resulting in the construction of new academic buildings and additional student hostels. In 1993, the college became affiliated with the National University of Bangladesh.

Today, Rangpur Government College offers HSC, Degree (Pass), and Honours programmes across multiple disciplines. Student organizations such as Rover Scout, BNCC, and Bandhon (Voluntary Blood Donors' Association) actively contribute to leadership development, community service, and humanitarian activities. Honours programmes began in the 1997–1998 academic session and continue to expand.

In 2011, undergraduate programmes were introduced in the Department of Mathematics alongside the Departments of English, Botany, and Zoology, marking an important milestone in the academic development of the college.
